This platform meters what its customers' own AWS estates do, and a business here now wants to charge for outbound network traffic: the bytes each customer's machines push out. Build the collection that turns that traffic into billable usage, on a five-minute schedule alongside the other metered resource kinds. Each run arrives with the role to assume inside the customer's account, the external id that role requires, the region to look in, and the identifier of the dimension being billed; the business comes with the run. Machines opt into metering by tag: one tag lists the dimensions a machine is metered on, comma separated, so one machine can be metered on several, and another names the customer it belongs to. A machine with no customer tag, or whose list does not include the dimension a run is for, is no part of that run. For every machine that is, establish how much traffic it sent out over the interval, bytes leaving the machine rather than arriving at it, and charge its customer the total it sent, in bytes, neither rounded nor converted to any larger unit. One customer may well be running several machines on the same dimension, and what each of them sent adds into that customer's single figure for the run. Power state has nothing to do with what is owed: a machine that has since been stopped or torn down still sent whatever it sent while it was up, and its customer owes for those bytes. Record the outcome the way the platform records every other kind of metered usage, as one figure per customer against the dimension the run is for, and make the resource kind selectable wherever the other metered kinds are, so a business can configure a dimension against it and have the scheduler dispatch to it. This task includes a local AWS-compatible emulator at the preconfigured AWS_ENDPOINT_URL. Its credentials and region are already in the shell, the repository's AWS SDK clients use it, and no real AWS account is involved. You may inspect its resources while developing and testing.
