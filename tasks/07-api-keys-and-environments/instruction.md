MeteringCo tenants integrate against two separate environments, a sandbox for trying things out and production for real traffic, and each is a separate account with its own customers, usage and billing behind it. A person signed in to the console works in one environment at a time and can move between them, and that choice governs the whole of the request that follows rather than only the sign-in decision: the account a request resolves to, the credentials listed back, and the data reachable through them all follow the environment the caller is currently in, so work done in the sandbox is invisible in production and a sandbox credential cannot read production. Build the console's API key screen on top of that. A tenant administrator should see the machine credentials their account holds in the environment they are in, ask for a fresh secret on one that may have leaked, and retire one for good when an integration is decommissioned. Rotating replaces the secret on that credential alone and leaves every other credential untouched. Retiring withdraws the credential at the identity provider and takes the account it signs in as out of the tenant's configuration, so a caller still presenting it is refused from that moment rather than at the next hour or the next deployment; moving between environments must likewise take effect on the very next request. A credential belongs to exactly one environment of one tenant, so a request naming one the current account does not hold must be refused and must leave that credential exactly as it was, whether it belongs to another tenant, to this tenant's other environment, to an integration already retired, or to something the identity provider knows about that the tenant never claimed. Rotating and retiring are administrative acts: a sign-in carrying only the right to read credentials may list them and do nothing else. This task includes a local emulator standing in for the identity provider and the configuration store at the preconfigured endpoint. Its credentials are already in the shell, the repository's clients use it, and no external account is involved. You may inspect it while developing and testing.
