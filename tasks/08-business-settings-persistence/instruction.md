A business keeps its own configuration with us in one document: the trading name and address that head an invoice, the support address customers write to, the payment terms, whether invoices go out on their own or wait for approval, whether they are raised per transaction or gathered up at the end of a cycle, whether we email them, and the wording and styling of the pages a customer lands on when they follow a payment link. Every screen in the console sends only what the person on it touched, and businesses keep telling support that correcting a postcode leaves their approval preference and their page wording sitting at values they never chose. We want a save to mean what people already assume it means: it carries the fields it names and nothing else, so anything the caller left out keeps whatever that business had stored, while a field the caller did send is written even when what they sent is blank, since emptying a line of an address is a thing people do deliberately and is not the same as never having filled it in. That holds inside the nested part of the document too. Naming one of those customer-facing pages must leave the other two exactly as they were, and naming one entry in a page's colour and spacing block must leave the rest of that block alone. One consequence of a save has to travel with it rather than sit beside it. A business can ask us to work its compute costs out from its own cluster, which only yields figures while the hourly collection behind it is running, so a save that turns that on for a business that did not have it should get that collection going, a save that turns it off should wind it down, and a save that simply restates what was already there should do neither. Lastly, the console screen that shows a business its own trading details can only display them at the moment, and the people using it expect to edit them there; it should be able to save that screen's own field set, over the same document, behaving the same way. This box runs local stand-ins for the cloud services and the configuration store the platform talks to, already wired into the environment, so you can inspect and exercise them freely while you work.
